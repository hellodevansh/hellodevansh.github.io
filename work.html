Warranty fraud detectionOla Electric · Product lead

Teaching a system to catch clever cheats

Warranty claims had grown past what anyone could review by hand, so most were getting auto-approved. Technicians noticed. They'd upload videos of working parts, get the replacement approved, and sell the part.

I led the team that built an agentic system to check every claim video against the defect standard for that specific part, using vision models. I designed the eval set myself from real claims and the edge cases technicians actually produced. Every time they found a new trick, we studied it and tightened the evals. It became a cat-and-mouse game, and the system got genuinely better because of it.

US$400k
saved every month, live across 300 service stores
What I'd say in a room: the eval set and the iteration loop are the real work. The model was the easy part.
Business Finance spendOla Electric · Ownership

No handover, so I drew my own map

Early on, people left the Business Finance team and the work landed on me. I didn't know the area, and there was no clean handover, just expense data and a function I'd never run.

So I broke the spend down vendor by vendor and activity by activity. I went straight to the business owners and asked what each expense was supposed to achieve. Two big levers came out of that, and with senior leaders I rationalised the spend and tightened the monthly controls so the savings would stick.

US$300k
a month in steady-state savings (about Rs 2.5 crore)
Agentic finance workflowsOla Electric · Builder

Four workflows, 50+ people, zero blind trust

I designed and shipped four agentic AI workflows across Corporate and Business Finance. They handle cross-document diligence, weekly management reporting and first-draft collateral.

The rule I kept: AI produces the draft, a person reviews it. That's what made people actually use these, rather than poke at them once and quietly go back to spreadsheets.

AI legal due-diligence agentOla Electric · Design

An agent that knows when to stop and ask

Diligence requests come in with changing instructions, shifting contacts, missing documents and a lot of follow-ups. A fixed workflow breaks the moment the next step depends on someone's reply.

So the agent tracks the owner, status, missing items and last action for every request. Deterministic code handles the predictable steps. A language model handles only the judgment calls. Anything uncertain or high-risk goes to a human. I'd rather the agent asks than guesses.

A refinancing that wouldn't closeOla Electric · Stakeholder work

Five doors closed. Then we changed the room.

We spent months trying to refinance a term loan. Most lenders passed, some after a month of paperwork, and nobody gave a clear reason. What I learned: a pass usually says more about the person in the room than the company. Those credit officers didn't have the appetite or authority to say yes.

So we changed the room. Senior leadership started speaking to the banks directly, and I set those conversations up. Three senior lender conversations are active now. The deal is still in evaluation, so I won't call it a win yet.
